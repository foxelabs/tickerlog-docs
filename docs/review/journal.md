# Journal

The Journal is where you write down *why*. One entry per trade: what you saw,
what you expected, and what you were feeling when you took it.

The numbers are recoverable from your broker at any time. This is the part that
is not.

## The layout

A list on the left, the selected entry on the right. Selecting an entry and then
paging the list keeps your selection — the detail pane does not empty out
underneath you.

## What to write

The entries that are worth reading back tend to answer three things:

- **What did you see?** The setup, as you read it at the time.
- **What did you expect?** Including where you were wrong.
- **What state were you in?** Tired, tilted, chasing, bored. This is the field
  people skip and then wish they had.

Write it at the time. An entry reconstructed a week later is a reconstruction of
what you *now* think you thought.

## Entries belong to trades

Each entry is attached to a trade, which is why **deleting a trade deletes its
entry**. An orphan note about a trade you can no longer see has nothing to be
about.

The delete confirmation on the Trades screen says this explicitly.

## Searching

The search box matches entry text. The account switcher scopes the list, like
every other screen.

## The pager

Journal uses the same pager as every other list — count line, page numbers, rows
per page — and it wraps onto two lines inside the narrow column rather than
having a reduced design of its own.
